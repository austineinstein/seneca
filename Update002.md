**The major public news driving crypto markets (and related risk assets) in recent days—primarily this week ending around 21–22 August 2026—centres on U.S. policy and liquidity signals that triggered a sharp risk-on rally, short squeezes, and institutional flows.**

### 1. U.S. Treasury expansion of long-dated bond buybacks
The U.S. Treasury (under Secretary Scott Bessent) announced it would at least double the size of liquidity-support buybacks of longer-dated nominal Treasury securities (mainly 10- to 30-year) from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation. The expanded programme is scheduled to run from early September through early November.  

This followed a sharp rise in long-term yields (the 30-year had reached levels not seen since around 2007). The move helped pull yields lower, supported perceptions of easier financial conditions / improved liquidity, and contributed to a weaker-dollar narrative. Analysts widely described it as a key macro catalyst that boosted risk appetite for assets like Bitcoin and Ethereum (even though it is not formal quantitative easing). Gold also rose on related debt and dollar concerns.

### 2. White House crypto engagement and push for the Clarity Act
President Donald Trump hosted a White House meeting/summit with crypto industry executives (including from firms such as Coinbase, Ripple, and others) and publicly urged Congress to pass a “fair version” of the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (often called the Clarity Act or CLARITY Act). The bill aims to establish a clearer U.S. regulatory framework for digital assets (including distinctions between securities and commodities and agency oversight).  

This provided a strong regulatory-optimism catalyst after the legislation had faced delays and sticking points (including ethics provisions). Related commentary included signals from the CFTC that it would move to establish rules using existing authorities if the bill continues to stall.

### 3. Strong institutional demand via spot ETFs
U.S. spot Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs recorded notable net inflows during the period (examples cited in reporting include roughly $500M+ days for Bitcoin products and ~$189–221M for Ethereum products on key sessions). These flows marked some of the stronger single-day or multi-day hauls in recent months and were viewed as evidence of renewed traditional-finance and institutional interest coinciding with the macro and policy news.

### 4. Massive short squeezes and liquidations
As prices broke higher, leveraged short positions were forcibly closed, creating a feedback loop. Reports indicated billions of dollars in crypto short liquidations over multi-day windows (with large single-day and multi-hour figures frequently cited in the $1B–$4B+ range across assets, heavily skewed toward shorts initially). This amplified the upward move in Bitcoin (which approached or tested levels near $78k–$80k at peaks) and especially Ethereum (which posted outsized percentage gains, often cited around +25% or more for the week in various windows). Later sessions also saw notable long liquidations during pullbacks and elevated volatility.

### Market impact summary
These factors converged to produce one of Bitcoin’s strongest weekly performances in years (roughly +20–25% in many reports) and an even stronger relative move in Ethereum and parts of the altcoin market. Overall crypto market capitalisation rose substantially (hundreds of billions in some tallies). The rally pulled crypto out of a prolonged period of relative stagnation/weakness, with supporting technical and on-chain elements (such as reduced exchange supply in some analyses) also noted.

Secondary or concurrent items included progress on filings such as Grayscale’s amended spot Zcash ETF application and various ecosystem developments, but the Treasury buyback signal, regulatory optimism around the Clarity Act/White House engagement, ETF flows, and the resulting short squeeze were the dominant public catalysts cited across major coverage.

Broader financial markets saw related effects (e.g., gold strength, some equity moves tied to risk sentiment and yields), while upcoming events such as Jackson Hole commentary and further fiscal/regulatory developments remain in focus. Markets remain volatile, with weekend liquidity and profit-taking noted as near-term considerations.
